I am an NLP Scientist at the National Board of Medical Examiners ([NBME](https://www.nbme.org/research/meet-our-experts)), where I work on automated scoring of clinical communication.

Most of my research is about measurement. Not "does the model score well," but the question underneath it: does this evaluation actually measure what it claims to measure, and how would we know if it didn't?

That question has become urgent. We increasingly evaluate models with other models — LLM judges, automated scorers, faithfulness metrics — and it is easy for such an evaluation to end up measuring the evaluator instead of the system under test. My recent work shows this happening in practice, and develops methods to separate the two. I care about building evaluations that survive their own audit.

I work across research and applied ML: benchmark and evaluation-set design, judge model training and validation, faithfulness auditing, fairness evaluation, and the psychometric methods that assessment has used for decades to establish that a measurement is trustworthy.

Before NBME I completed my PhD in Computer Science at the University of Georgia under [Sheng Li](https://sheng-li.org/). During my doctoral work and internships at Adobe Research and Reuters, my research centered on improving machine comprehension by integrating auxiliary knowledge into language models.

Outside of work I play the setar and shoot street photography.
