I'm an NLP research scientist at [NBME](https://www.nbme.org/research/meet-our-experts), where I work on automated scoring of how doctors communicate with patients.

Most of what I do comes down to one question: when we say a model scored well, did we actually measure the thing we meant to? It's a harder question than it sounds, especially now that we mostly evaluate models using other models. A lot of my work is about telling those two apart — what the model did, and what the evaluation did to it. There's more on the [research]({{ site.github.url }}/pages/research.html) page.

I like working here because the scores matter. They feed into how physicians get assessed, which means there are experts checking the work and real pressure to get it right. That turns out to be a good place to find out whether a method actually holds up.

Before NBME I did my PhD at the University of Georgia with [Sheng Li](https://sheng-li.org/), with stints at Adobe Research and Reuters along the way, mostly on ways to feed outside knowledge into language models.

Outside of work I play the [setar]({{ site.github.url }}/pages/media.html) and shoot street photography.
